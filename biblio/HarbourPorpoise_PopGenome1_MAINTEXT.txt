Abstract from the SMM2026Harbor porpoises (Phocoena phocoena) are among the smallest coastal cetaceans across the Northern Hemisphere. Despite reports of multiple subspecies and ecotypes across the North Atlantic and North Pacific, their evolutionary history remains poorly understood. Here, we analyzed 109 whole genomes (average sequencing depth of 12±8x), including 100 harbor porpoises from 17 localities in the North Atlantic and North Pacific, six DallÕs porpoises (Phocoenoides dalli), their closest relatives, and three outgroups. We identified ~4 million high quality SNPs in their genomes, enabling analyses of population structure, connectivity, demographic history, admixture, and introgression events among subspecies, and with the DallÕs porpoises. The widespread P. p. phocoena subspecies in the North Atlantic exhibited high genetic diversity and evidence of population expansion, in contrast to the localized Afro-Iberian upwelling porpoises (possibly P. p. meridionalis), and P. p. relicta in the Black Sea. These two local subspecies displayed high levels of homozygosity across their genomes and evidence of recent inbreeding, especially for Black Sea porpoises. The North Pacific harbor porpoises (P. p. vomerina) displayed comparably high, yet slightly lower, diversity than the North Atlantic P. p. phocoena. Admixture-graph and coalescent-based analyses revealed that the high diversity of the P. p. phocoena did not stem only from its widespread distribution range, but also from repeated historical secondary contacts and admixture events with the North Pacific P. p. vomerina during the Quaternary Glaciations. The genomes from three North-Western Pacific harbor porpoises (from Japan and Bering Sea) were strikingly distinct from others, as they displayed significant signs of introgression from the DallÕs porpoises, and maybe also from an other unsampled species (possibly the finless porpoise). This study provides the first global genomic assessment on the diversification processes among harbor porpoise subspecies and their close relatives. Our findings highlight a rich and complex evolutionary history in porpoises, underlining how porous species boundaries are, and bring significant conservation implications.Snippet for mediaFirst global genomic assessment of the evolution and diversification processes among harbor porpoise subspecies and their close relative, the Dall's porpoise, highlights a rich and complex evolutionary history, and how porous species boundaries are.Introduction Who: MCFResultsDiscussionMaterial and Methods1. Sampling and laboratory procedure (MCF)Tissue samples from 26 harbour porpoises and six Dall porpoises were obtained from by-caught or stranded animals (Table 1 and Table S1), placed in salt-saturated 20% DMSO or 95% Ethanol and stored at ??20 ¡C until analyses. All samples were collected under appropriate Marine Mammal Protection Act permits within the US, or appropriate permits elsewhere, following the relevant guidelines and regulations, and transferred internationally under CITES permit. Genomic DNA was extracted from tissues using the PureGene or DNeasy Tissue kits (Qiagen), following the manufacturerÕs recommendations. DNA quality and quantity were assessed on an agarose gel stained with ethidium bromide, as well as using a Qubit-v3 fluorometer. Genomic libraries for the 14 North Pacific harbor porpoises and the six Dall's porpoises were prepared by Swift Biosciences Inc. using either the Swift Biosciences Accel-NGS double-strand 2S (harbour porpoises) or single-strand 1S Plus DNA Library Kit (DallÕs porpoise), following the user protocol and targeting an insert-size of ~?350 base-pairs (bps). Additional genome libraries for 12 samples of North Atlantic harbor porpoises were prepared at BGI Inc. using their proprietary protocol. Both types of libraries were sequenced for 150 bps paired-end on an Illumina HiSeq-4000 sequencer at BGI Inc., targeting a whole genome sequencing average depth of ca. 5x. Short read data archives from 74 additional harbor porpoises from the North Atlantic and Black Sea previously generated by (Celemin et al., 2023) were retrieved from NCBI. These samples were previously sequenced using 2 x 150bps paired-end on an Illumina NovaSeq-6000 targeting an average depth of ca. 10x. Overall the genomic data from 100 harbor porpoise samples covered 17 localities in the North Pacific, North Atlantic and adjacent waters (Figure 1).Genomic data from three outgroup species were also included in this study to root phylogenetic trees and polarize ancestral vs. derived allelic states. These outgroups included one narwhal (Monodon monoceros), one beluga (Delphinapterus leucas), and one killer whale (Orcinus orca) (Table S2).Figure 1. Sampling distribution map       2.
